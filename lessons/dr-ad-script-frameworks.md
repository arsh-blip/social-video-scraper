# Video Scripting Frameworks → 30–60s Direct-Response Ads

Distilled from the two Youri van Hofwegen videos in `../scraped_videos/`:

- `1wrYP4ayp4I` — *This AI Writes Better Scripts than 99% of YouTubers*
- `jaOIw-NiEPM` — *EXACTLY How to Write INSANELY Good YouTube Scripts with AI*

Both are pitched at long-form YouTube (3–15 min), but the underlying psychology
travels. This doc extracts the reusable parts and rewrites them for the
constraints of a 30–60s performance DR ad (Meta / TikTok / Shorts / YouTube
in-stream).

---

## 1. Frameworks worth stealing

### 1a. Pattern-Interrupt Curiosity Hook (Video 1)
Three-beat opener:

1. **Unbelievable stat or question** — kills the scroll. *"Did you know 91% of YouTube videos never reach 1,000 views?"*
2. **Pre-empt the objection** — strip away the "this isn't for me" reason. *"…without fancy equipment or years of experience."*
3. **Information gap** — name a specific, countable thing they have to keep watching to learn. *"5 simple techniques."*

### 1b. Confirm-the-Click + Curiosity Loop (Video 2)
The hook does two jobs:

- **Confirm the click** — the first 2s must reassure the viewer that this is the thing the thumbnail/title promised. Wrong promise = instant bounce.
- **Open a loop** — make a payoff promise you cash in later. The gap between "what they know" and "what they're waiting to find out" is what holds them.

The full hook formula in Video 2:
*Immediate content reconfirming the click → credibility proof → a twist they haven't heard before → why it matters → planted tension toward the payoff.*

### 1c. Per-Section Content Beat (Video 1)
Each body section follows the same five-step pattern:

1. Identify a common problem/misconception.
2. Create emotional connection ("imagine you're staring at the garden center, completely overwhelmed…") — they call this **emotional transportation**.
3. Reveal a surprising insight or solution.
4. Give specific actionable advice.
5. **Quantify the result** with a number.

### 1d. Bridge Statements + Rehooks (both videos)
Curiosity decays the longer you talk. Every ~30–40s, hit a reset:

- *"But here's where it gets really interesting…"*
- *"And if you skip this next step, everything else falls apart."*
- *"Let me ask you this — have you ever tried…?"*

These re-open a loop right before the natural drop-off point.

### 1e. Emotional Variety / Waves (Video 2)
Don't run a flat tonal line. Cycle through fear → surprise → relief → humor.
Zoom in on a detail, zoom out to the big picture, zoom back in. Pattern-break
every ~30–40s with a new example, question, or shift in pace.

### 1f. Future-Pacing + Outro (both videos)
Endings always:

1. Recap the value ("there you have it, the 5 techniques…").
2. Visualize the outcome ("you'll get a harvest without wasting months on dead plants and empty soil") — concrete, sensory, owned.
3. Name the **next problem** they'll hit ("but now you've got 100k tomatoes — what do you do with them?").
4. Point them at the action that solves it.

The "name the next problem" move is the most underused trick — it makes the CTA feel inevitable, not bolted on.

### 1g. Three-Point Preview
After the hook: *"In this video I'll show you (1) why X is failing, (2) the exact method I used, (3) how to do it in five minutes."* Useful in long-form. Mostly **too heavy for ≤60s ads** unless the offer is complex.

## 2. What does NOT translate to a 30–60s DR ad

| YouTube long-form | DR ad reality |
|---|---|
| 15s to land the hook | 1–2s. If they're still scrolling at frame 60, you lost. |
| Session-time / next-video CTA | Irrelevant. One CTA, one URL, one offer. |
| 3-point preview after hook | Burns 5–8s of a 30s ad. Skip unless 60s+. |
| Mimicking your "personal voice" | DR voice is its own register: certain, urgent, specific. Personality second. |
| 21-page guide as the "moat" | Collapse the moat into a single named mechanism ("the 4-second hook test", "the offset pour method"). |
| Slow credibility build | Credibility lives inside the hook itself, not after it. |
| Curiosity loops that pay off 8 minutes later | Loops must close inside the same ad. Long loops = abandoned cart. |

## 3. Adapted framework: the 30–60s DR Ad Beat Sheet

Compressed version of Youri's structure, with timing budgets for a 45s ad:

```
0–2s    HOOK              Pattern-interrupt + confirm-the-click
2–6s    PROBLEM + STAKES  Named pain, emotional transportation
6–12s   CREDIBILITY       1 proof point in the same breath as the mechanism
12–25s  MECHANISM REVEAL  The "twist" — the named, specific thing they haven't heard
25–35s  PROOF STACK       Numbers, before/after, social proof, objection knockdown
35–42s  FUTURE-PACE       Visualize the outcome, sensory + concrete
42–45s  CTA               Name the next obstacle → the offer is the solution
```

Per-beat rules (lifted from the videos and tightened for DR):

- **Every 8–10s, rehook.** *"And here's the kicker…", "But that's not even the best part…"* — same psychology as the long-form rehook, just at 4× the cadence.
- **Quantify everything.** "Better sleep" → "fell asleep in 7 minutes". The Algo Script method's per-section pattern requires a number.
- **One mechanism, one name.** Pick a single proprietary phrase and repeat it. ("the 4-second hook test", "the algo script method"). This is what makes the offer feel like IP, not a commodity.
- **Objection pre-emption inside the hook.** Same beat as Youri's *"…without expensive equipment or years of experience"* — except in DR you're killing "this is too expensive / I've tried this before / I don't have time."
- **CTA = next-problem framing**, not "click below." *"…but the cheap version sells out in 48h, so —"* > *"Click the link now."*

## 4. Two worked examples

### Example A — 30s DR ad for a sleep supplement

```
0–2s   Hook
       "73% of people who 'sleep 8 hours' wake up exhausted —
       and it's not insomnia."

2–7s   Problem + emotional transportation
       "You know the feeling. Alarm goes off, you've technically
       slept, but it's like your brain never got the memo."

7–13s  Credibility + mechanism reveal (one breath)
       "A Stanford team found the issue isn't sleep length —
       it's a magnesium drop in stage-3 sleep. We built one
       capsule that fixes only that window."

13–22s Proof stack
       "12,000 customers. Average 7 minutes to fall asleep.
       4.8 stars. Refund rate under 2%."

22–27s Future-pace
       "Imagine waking up before the alarm, actually
       wanting to get out of bed."

27–30s CTA with next-obstacle framing
       "Tonight's batch ships free — link below before it
       goes back to paid shipping Monday."
```

### Example B — 45s DR ad for a B2B SaaS (cold outreach tool)

```
0–2s   "Cold email is dead — unless you know the one rule
        Google's spam filter changed in 2026."

2–8s   "If you're sending from a connected Gmail or Outlook,
        your reply rates have quietly collapsed in the last
        90 days. You probably haven't even noticed."

8–16s  Mechanism — name it.
        "We call it the 'warm-tier loophole'. Every email
        gets routed through verified primary inboxes that
        Google already trusts."

16–28s Proof stack + objection knockdown
        "412 agencies switched in Q1. Average reply rate
        went from 1.1% to 6.4%. And no, it's not Smartlead —
        we tested against them. Screenshots in the comments."

28–37s Future-pace + rehook
        "Picture Monday morning: 40 booked calls instead of
        4 'unsubscribe' replies. That's the difference one
        deliverability tier makes."

37–45s CTA with next-obstacle framing
        "We're capping new accounts at 200 this month so
        the inbox pool doesn't burn. Link below — if it
        says 'waitlist', you missed it."
```

Both examples follow the same skeleton: pattern-interrupt stat → emotional
moment → named mechanism → quantified proof → future-pace → CTA-as-next-problem.

## 5. Checklist before shipping a DR script

- [ ] Hook is ≤2s and contains a number, a pain, or a named villain.
- [ ] Click-confirm: a viewer who clicked because of the thumbnail/headline sees their reason validated in the first sentence.
- [ ] Mechanism is **named** (a phrase, not a description) and repeated.
- [ ] At least one number per 10s of runtime.
- [ ] One rehook every 8–10s (*"but here's the catch…"*).
- [ ] Future-pace beat is sensory and concrete, not abstract ("wake up before the alarm" > "feel more rested").
- [ ] CTA frames the *next* obstacle, not the click. Urgency is real, not invented.
- [ ] No three-point preview (unless ≥60s).
- [ ] No "personal voice" warmth at the cost of certainty — DR voice trades nuance for conviction.
- [ ] Tonal variety: at least 2 emotional shifts in 30s, 3 in 60s.

## 6. Open questions for our use case

- **Vertical-specific hook libraries.** Youri's "91% never reach 1,000 views" works because the audience self-identifies. We'd want a small bank of pattern-interrupt stats per vertical (sleep, finance, B2B SaaS, etc.) — worth building from scraped competitor ads.
- **Voice cloning vs. DR voice.** Video 1's "my style" group is great for creator content but actively hurts DR. We probably want the opposite: a "DR voice" reference group built from top-performing ads in the niche, not from the founder's existing content.
- **Mechanism naming.** This is the single highest-leverage move and it's mostly absent from generic AI ad scripts. Worth a dedicated prompt step: *"Invent a named mechanism for this offer that sounds proprietary."*
